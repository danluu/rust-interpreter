# Scope native allocator transitions before implementing a bridge

Start from exact adopted fca687eb runtime sources. The heap HashMap candidate is
parked after its closed primary failed the wall margin; do not compose or retime
it. Reuse only closed current-host adopted profiles and current ordinary-entropy
sample windows. No guest, compiler, executable publication or timing command.

Count the seven allocator opcodes using the independently qualified original-PC
counter decoder. Require all such executions to be interpreted. Record active
sites and whether the following PC has an emitted native entry. Separately join
the complete saved native self-sample sites to allocator-adjacent regions: entry/
range guards after an allocator operation, and flush/exit before one. Keep all
counts disjoint; show the broader native-boundary and other-host sample buckets
without assuming they all belong to allocation. Heap implementation work remains
part of a host allocator call and cannot be counted as eliminated by a bridge.

The frequency of interpreted allocation operations is not latency or a speedup
estimate. Partial ordinary-entropy samples and complete fixed-entropy logical
profiles remain different measurements. This is a scope diagnostic only, not
an ABI/lifetime/budget/fault proof or implementation admission. Any later bridge
must refresh relocated heap bases, preserve live registers and guest state,
exact errors/limits/counters and every instruction-budget prefix, and pass the
original full changed-source gates. No host allocator address becomes a guest
pointer and no guest check or strict Rust frontend check may be skipped.

no new modeled optimization or new unit fixtures are needed for weighted counts.
